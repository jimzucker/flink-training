Proving Apache Flink scales linearly

We built a trading pipeline on Apache Flink and Kafka — block trades in, positions and market value out — and measured exactly what each additional core buys.

[LEAD IMAGE: docs/img/scaling-linear.png]

Block trades handled, per second:

• 1 core — 30,505
• 2 cores — 65,721
• 4 cores — 129,056

Four cores do 4.23× the work of one. Straight line, no cliff. That is a capacity model you can budget against — you know what the next core buys before you buy it.

And it is real work: every trade fans out to five downstream updates, so at four cores the pipeline is writing 645,280 records a second.

All of it on a laptop, against a single Kafka broker.

Claude Code built all of it — the pipeline, the tests, the monitoring, the benchmark behind these numbers. Twenty hours of build in total — six of those were my time at the keyboard.

The scaling numbers come from scripts/scale-units.sh, which caps the task manager's cores and holds everything else still — same single broker, eight partitions, the same 50,000,000-order backlog drained with the producer stopped. It records what Flink actually used at each step (1.00, 2.00 and 3.94 of the 1, 2 and 4 cores), which is what shows the cores were the variable rather than something that happened to correlate.

Worth saying plainly: this is one pipeline — a keyed split, two aggregations, exactly-once Kafka sinks — not a claim about every Flink job. A workload with heavy state or skewed keys could look different.

docs/steps/step-12/scaling-demo.md has the full write-up, including where it stops: push past four cores on this laptop and a single broker's write path becomes the ceiling, at which point you are measuring Kafka, not Flink.
