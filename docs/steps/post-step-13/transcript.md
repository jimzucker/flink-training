Because they're **two separate Flink jobs**, not one — and that's a real design choice, not a rendering artifact.

`MarketValueJob` reads sinks ③ and ④ **back out of Kafka** as its own sources, rather than receiving the positions streams in-process:

```java
env.fromSource(source(config, positionsTopic, "mv-" + positionsTopic), ...)
```

So Part 1 writes `positions-by-symbol` / `positions-by-account` to Kafka, and Part 2 reads those topics fresh. Flink draws one graph per job, so you get two.

##### Why it's built that way

The assignment names two parts, and sinks ③ and ④ are required deliverables — those topics must exist and be correct regardless of what consumes them. Reading them back is the honest version of that: Part 2 consumes exactly what any other consumer would, so if the topics are wrong, Part 2 is visibly wrong too. It also means either part can be restarted, rescaled or replayed without touching the other.

The cost is a Kafka round-trip between them, and the extra latency that adds.

##### They could be one

Technically yes — the positions streams could feed the market-value operators directly in memory while still being written to Kafka for the deliverable. One job, one graph, no round-trip. What you'd lose is the independence and the property that Part 2 validates the published topics rather than trusting an in-process stream.

I'd flag this as worth a decision before the deck goes out: **a slide showing two graphs invites exactly the question you just asked.** Either the slide says why in a line, or it shows only Part 1 — the positions pipeline — which is the one the demo narrative actually walks through.

Which would you prefer? And note this is documented nowhere in the repo — I looked, and the rationale above is reconstructed from the code, not quoted from a design note. That's a gap worth closing either way.
