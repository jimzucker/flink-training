At p≥2 every keyed record has to be routed and shipped. You pay that once, going from 1 to 2, and never again; from then on doubling just adds marginal cost, which is why 2→4→8 improve.

So the 1-unit point is measuring a *structurally different pipeline* — it's not one-eighth of the 8-unit configuration, it's a pipeline with the shuffle removed. It flatters the baseline and makes every subsequent step look worse than it is.

Also worth noting: I offered hyperthreading as the likely cause before running this, and the repeat rules it out — a scheduling-placement artifact would not land within 1.7% twice. `lscpu` does confirm 8 physical cores / 16 threads, so the box is as described; that just isn't what's driving this.
